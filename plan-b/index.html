DAY 0 Batch journal · Chennai hatchery

Thirty days from egg to aquarium.

Every clownfish we sell begins here — a copper-orange clutch laid beside a broodstock pair, fanned all night by a fastidious father. This page follows one batch, day by day, through the living cultures that raise it.

DAY 7 Hatch night

They hatch in the dark, and swim to the light.

An hour after sunset, the clutch lets go. Larvae the size of an eyelash rise to the surface, steering toward any glow — in the ocean, moonlight; in our hatchery, a dim lamp over the larval tank. Bring your light close and watch them gather.

What feeds this stage

Live rotifers

A larva's first meal must be alive, moving, and smaller than its eye. Our rotifer cultures are the same ones swirling in that tank — enriched on live phyto, harvested to order.

DAY 10 Greenwater

The water itself is alive.

We tint every larval tank green with live phytoplankton. It softens the light, feeds the rotifers around the clock, and keeps water quality steady — the old hatchery trick the ocean invented first. Under the loupe, greenwater isn't green at all: it's billions of separate cells.

What feeds this stage

Live phytoplankton

Dense, culture-fresh microalgae. Feed your pods and rotifers, tint a fry tank, or treat your pod cultures to a plankton bloom.

DAY 18 Metamorphosis

The stripes arrive like a signature.

Over a few remarkable days, a silver sliver becomes a clownfish. The head bar appears first, then the body bar, then the tail — each band inked in over hours. Hover each stage to watch the bands come in.

DAY 30 Tank-ready

Thirty days old and already eating everything.

By the time a juvenile leaves us it has weaned from rotifers onto prepared foods, seen aquarium life from its first day, and never touched a reef. That's the whole point: a hardier fish, and a wild pair left where they belong.
